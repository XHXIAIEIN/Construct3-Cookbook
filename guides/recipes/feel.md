# Feel

The table below applies the Native first table of
[design/thinking.md](../design/thinking.md) to the effects that the
player notices first. Before you add the sounds for these effects, read
[sound.md](sound.md). If the effect belongs to an attack, read
[attack-feel.md](attack-feel.md): it covers the wind-up, the animation,
cancelling, the projectile, the sounds, the hit effect and the damage
numbers.

Each row names its source in the Example column:

- an official example's id;
- `observed`: what a game project settled in its r504 preview, 2026-09-30
  to 2026-10-05, or what a probe project showed in the stable editor's
  preview on 2026-10-08, recorded in `docs/decisions/count-up-and-press.md`
  of the Construct3-Agent-Skills repository, or what a probe project showed
  in the r506.3 preview at 144 Hz on 2026-10-26;
- `studied`: a mechanism read in a studied project's sheets, 2026-10-06;
- `measured`: a probe project played in a preview at 60 and 144 Hz, with
  its numbers in `docs/decisions/borrowed-numbers.md` of the
  Construct3-Agent-Skills repository.

| Need | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| Screen shake on impact | Use Scroll To *Shake* with *Reducing magnitude*, and take the duration from the effect (`Timeline.TotalTime(tag)`). The event sets the magnitude: about 0.5, 1 and 2 % of the viewport's short side for a crit, a merge and a kill | cave-bridge, three-cups; observed |
| Hit stop, slow motion | *Set time scale* 0.1, *Wait*, *Set time scale* 1. A *Tween (value)* that feeds *Set time scale* gives a smooth ramp. A scheduled sound still plays on time, but a timeline with *Use system timescale* on stops with the game ([pitfalls: Timeline controller](../plugins/timeline.md)) | segmented-boss-fight, samuroof, eventide |
| Squash, pop, bounce on impact | Tween *Size* or *X Scale*/*Y Scale* from `On collision`. *Ping pong* makes a pop that returns, and an *In Back* ease makes a wind-up | gold-mining, cannon-launch, gravity-portal |
| Squash that fires again before the last one has returned | Record the rest width and height at creation. Set the deformation at once and tween back to the rest size, so a repeat never shifts the rest size. The ease shows the material: `easeoutelastic` over 0.5 s wobbles like jelly, and `easeoutback` over 0.3 s bounces once like something hard | observed |
| Several effects on one body at once: squash, breath, charge, lift, flash, a lunge | Make each effect a *Tween (value)* channel from its full amount to 0. Every tick, recompute the body's size, position, angle and brightness from rest, as a product or a sum of the channels. So a new effect starts while the last one still fades, and nothing jumps. A channel that has ended reads 0 and has no effect ([pitfalls: Tween](../behaviors/tween.md)). An idle breath is Sine in value-only mode, read into the same sum, so only the sum sets the Size property | observed |
| A keyframed motion of one part: a weapon swing, a wind-up and strike, a skill | Put a Timeline on the part, which is a hierarchy child, and give it *Relative* tracks that start and end at 0. So the timeline adds to what the parent's channels do, and it repeats without shifting. Each instance has a copy of the timeline, tagged with the instance's UID. Put the weight in custom Eases from the Eases folder on each keyframe, not in numbers in events. Use *Set time* 0 before a replay, and also to take a finished timeline's offsets off the part ([pitfalls: Timeline controller](../plugins/timeline.md)) | observed |
| A score or a currency that counts to its new value | *Stop* the count's tag, then *Tween (value)* from the number that the text shows, `int(Text.Text)`, to the new value over 0.5 s with `easeoutquad`. Under *Is playing*, set the text to the value rounded towards the target: `ceil` when it counts up and `floor` when it counts down, so a gain of 1 shows at once. In *On finished*, set the exact value, because the value reads 0 once the tween ends ([pitfalls: Tween](../behaviors/tween.md)). The generator template's `count_up()` and `counting()` do this | dig-the-way, tick-tock-toppings; observed |
| A button that presses under the finger | When a touch starts on the button, mark the button pressed and show it pressed at once: a *Pressed* frame, or its size set to 0.9 of its image. When any touch ends, tween the pressed button's size back in 0.1 s with `easeoutsine`. Run the button's action under *Is touching* the button, so a finger that slides off cancels the press. A label that is a hierarchy child of the button follows the button's width and height only if the link says so, and then the label's letters scale too. The generator template's `press()` does this | fuji-leaper, grukkle-onslaught, chess-checkmate; observed |
| A pickup waiting to be collected: a coin, a gem, a key | Use Sine *Vertical* with a period of 1 to 2 s and a magnitude of 5 to 15 % of the pickup's height. For a pickup that pulses, use Sine *Size* with the same period and the same share of its size | template-invert-gravity, kiwi-story, relic-hunter, balloon-blower |
| A jump just after walking off a ledge (coyote time), and a jump pressed just before landing (a jump buffer) | The player has Platform, a Timer and a boolean `JumpUsed`. Two globals of 0.1 s, `CoyoteTime` and `BufferTime`, hold the windows, and these are the variables to tune. The events, in order: 1. *On jump* sets `JumpUsed`. 2. *On fall* with `JumpUsed` not set starts timer "coyote" for `CoyoteTime`. 3. The jump key's *On key pressed*, on the floor: *Simulate control* Jump. 4. Else, while "coyote" runs: *Set vector Y* to `-Self.Platform.JumpStrength`, set `JumpUsed` and stop "coyote", because a simulated jump does nothing in the air ([pitfalls: Touch](../plugins/touch.md)). 5. Else: start timer "buffer" for `BufferTime`. 6. *On landed* clears `JumpUsed` and stops "coyote"; while "buffer" runs, it simulates Jump and stops "buffer". Both windows hold to within one tick at 60 and 144 Hz. A press after the top of a jump does not jump again | measured |
| Hit feedback | Flash *Flash* from `On collision`; Tween *Color* to `HexColor("#RRGGBB")` and back; or a Brightness effect, set to its peak at once and tweened back to 100. A game built from the skill's generator template shows a hit as a frame instead, with `hit_frame()` and `hit()`. Its `check_look.py` refuses Flash (`motion.hit`) | bewitched-torches, turret-predictive-aim; pinball, shifting-dungeon; observed |
| Telegraph what a drop will do | While the dragged instance is over a target that will take it, the target wobbles (two sines summed into its angle) and brightens, and the slot shows its highlight frame. The slot is larger than the piece, so the highlight shows around the piece | observed |
| A dragged thing that lags and overshoots like a spring | An invisible base takes Drag & Drop, the drop test and the snap. The visible parts are hierarchy children of a follower, which follows the base on a damped spring stepped every tick. The parts lean by how far they lag. Step the spring implicitly: compute the acceleration from the lag, then divide the velocity by `1 + damping·dt + stiffness·dt²`. Otherwise the lag differs between 30 and 144 fps. Do not move the base itself, because that breaks the drag: Drag & Drop writes the base's position only while the pointer moves | observed |
| A choreographed sequence over several objects: opening, level clear, a bridge rebuilding | Use Timeline *Play*, and *Set instance* for objects created at runtime. Put branches that must start together in sibling sub-events, because a *Wait* delays the sub-events after it | cave-bridge, 17 examples; observed |
| Camera that follows, clamped to a zone | If plain following is enough, put Scroll To on the target. For bounds and smoothing, use System *Scroll to position* with `lerp(scrollx, clamp(target, zone edges), …)` every tick | dynamic-camera-system |
| Camera that looks ahead of a running player | Every tick, set an invisible camera object with Scroll To to `lerp(Camera.X, target, 1 - exp(-k * dt))`, where `target` is `Player.X + Player.Platform.VectorX * (LOOKAHEAD / Player.Platform.MaxSpeed + 1 / k)`. An eased camera trails a target that moves at speed v by v / k, and the `1 / k` term adds that distance back. At 480 px/s, a lead of 160 px without the term showed 38 px at k = 4 and 98 px at k = 8; with it, 158 px. `1 - exp(-k * dt)` eases the same at any frame rate, while a fixed share per tick is stiffer at a higher frame rate | observed |
| A chapter or stage title card | The card runs in this order: dim, banner, a title that drops and lands, a shake with a burst on the landing, a hold, then everything leaves together. One tuned set of numbers follows. Dim the play area to 55 % opacity in 0.2 s. Open a banner from the centre: tween its width from 0 in 0.25 s with `easeoutquart`. 0.15 s after the card's first line appears, the title drops from 2.8 times its rest scale to 1 in 0.22 s with `easeinquad`, and it fades in as it falls. On landing, it squashes to 0.88 and springs back with `easeoutback` over 0.35 s. The screen shakes 6 px for 0.35 s, and stars burst from the title. 1.8 s after the card opened, the title, the banner and the dim rise and fade in 0.35 s with `easeinquad`. The next stage enters while they leave. Each phase of the title's scale is a *Tween (value)* channel, and the channels are multiplied every tick ([pitfalls: Tween](../behaviors/tween.md)) | observed |
| Warn before the hit: a slash, a beam, a falling block | Create a warning sprite at the attack's place and angle, and *Start timer* for the warning time. *On timer* creates the attack there, copies the attack's numbers from the warning and destroys the warning. When the fading attack's opacity falls under a threshold, set its damage to 0 before the attack is destroyed, so its last faint frames hit nothing | studied |
| An arena box that resizes, moves and turns as cues ask | Use one invisible manager sprite with a *Tween (value)* for each edge and for X, Y and the angle. Each edge's duration is its distance divided by one shared pixel speed, so every edge moves at the same speed and the farthest edge arrives last. Under *Is playing*, each edge reads its `Tween.Value(tag)`. *On finished* snaps the edge to its target and sets a latch. When every latch is set, a stored callback runs once ([scripted-sequences.md](scripted-sequences.md), "A cue that waits"). Derive the border sprites, the clip masks and the edge globals every tick from those few numbers (rule 7 of the design guide) | studied |
| Fade between layouts | Put a *Fader* sprite on the parallax-0 layer. Tween its opacity, then *Wait for previous actions* and *Go to layout* | avalanche, airborne-explorer |
